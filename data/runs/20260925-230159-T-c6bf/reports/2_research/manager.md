**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: The bull made a credible case on AT&T's stability: TTM FCF around $17.7B, a ~4.4% dividend covered at roughly 45% of FCF, a low ~0.43 beta, improving operating margin near 23%, and a ~4% annual share-count shrink. But the bear carried the decisive points. The bull's FCF-cushion math was wrong: annualizing the H1 buyback pace puts capital returns near $17.3B against ~$17.7B TTM FCF, and in H1 itself dividends plus buybacks (~$8.64B) exceeded H1 FCF (~$7.85B). That means the buyback is partly debt-supported, not purely organic. Leverage is at management's ~2.5x target with no cushion, gross debt is rising, interest expense is up ~14% YoY, and forward EPS of ~$2.57 sits below the gain-flattered TTM figure — leaving ~9.9x forward earnings on a ~2% grower with a PEG above 1. The capex "coiled spring" is a hope, not a guided inflection, and the competitive environment remains a grinder. On balance the bear case is stronger: the valuation is not mispriced, and the margin of safety the bull relies on is thinner than presented. However, this is not a Sell — TTM FCF still covers the dividend with room if buybacks are cut, coverage ratios are investment-grade, and the subscriber cash flows are defensive.

**Strategic Actions**: Trim AT&T to an Underweight position relative to a standard allocation. If a standard allocation is, for example, 3%, reduce toward 1.5%-2.0%. Execute by selling into strength around the 200-day average (~$25.15) or the upper end of the recent range rather than chasing; do not add on this valuation. Keep any remaining position for income and defensiveness, but disable dividend reinvestment if the goal is reduction. Revisit if one of the following shows up in reported results: buybacks are cut enough that capital return falls clearly below FCF, leverage trends below the 2.5x target, interest expense stabilizes, or management gives concrete capex-peak guidance. If capital returns continue to exceed FCF and net debt keeps rising, reduce further toward zero.
