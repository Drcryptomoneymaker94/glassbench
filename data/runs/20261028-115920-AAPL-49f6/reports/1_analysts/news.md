## 1. Executive Summary

Apple Inc. (AAPL) is navigating a complex macroeconomic backdrop characterized by **rising interest rates**, **persistent inflation**, and **regulatory headwinds**. The stock faces near-term valuation pressure from AI product skepticism, regulatory fines in key markets, and bearish sentiment around its premium pricing strategy. However, the broader macro environment suggests a soft landing is still the base case, with low recession probabilities and continued economic growth supporting long-term consumer demand for Apple's ecosystem.

## 2. Macroeconomic Environment

### Interest Rates & Fed Policy
- **Federal Funds Rate:** Currently at **3.75%** (as of September 2026), down from 4.09% a year ago — the Fed has been cutting rates, but with a slight uptick in September suggesting potential caution.
- **10-Year Treasury Yield:** Surged to **5.00%** as of October 26, up **+101 bps** from 3.99% a year ago — this is a significant rate increase that pressures equity valuations, especially for growth/tech stocks like AAPL.
- **Yield Curve (10Y-2Y):** At **0.29%** — still positive but compressed and trending toward inversion territory. The curve has narrowed significantly from 0.52% a year ago (-44%), signaling growing economic uncertainty.

### Inflation
- **CPI:** Latest reading at **336.298** (September 2026), up **+3.46%** year-over-year — inflation remains above the Fed's 2% target and is accelerating, not decelerating.
- **Core PCE:** At **130.455** (August 2026), up **+2.59%** YoY — still elevated above the Fed's 2% target, though less aggressive than headline CPI.

### Labor Market
- **Unemployment Rate:** At **4.2%** (September 2026), relatively stable from a year ago (4.5%) but ticking up slightly in recent months — labor market remains tight but showing early signs of softening.

### GDP Growth
- **Real GDP:** +1.17% growth from Q3 2025 to Q1 2026 — modest but positive growth, consistent with a soft landing scenario.

### Recession Risk
- **Polymarket probability of US recession by end of 2026: 7%** — low but not negligible; the market is pricing in a relatively stable economy.
