
Bull Analyst: # Bull Case for KO: The Market Is Pricing a Slow Grower. The Data Says Otherwise.

Let me open by being direct about where I think the bear camp gets KO wrong — because I can already hear the argument coming.

The bear thesis on KO writes itself: *"26x earnings, 4.0 PEG, yield below its historical average, insiders dumping stock at 52-week highs, and a dividend that wasn't covered by free cash flow last year. It's a great company at a full price — go buy it on a pullback."*

I've read that script. And here's the problem: it's a **snapshot thesis being applied to a business that is visibly inflecting in real time.** The bear is looking at the trailing twelve months. I'm looking at where this company is going — and the Q2'26 print is not the P&L of a 2% grower.

Let me make the case.

---

## 1. The Growth Reacceleration Is the Whole Ballgame — and the Bear Is Ignoring It

Let's go to the numbers, because they're striking:

- **Q2'26 revenue: $13.38B, +6.7% YoY** — versus the FY22→FY25 revenue CAGR of ~3.7%. That's nearly *double* the trend rate.
- **Q2'26 diluted EPS: $1.03 vs $0.88, +17.0% YoY** — the strongest quarter in the entire six-quarter dataset.
- **Forward EPS of $3.53** implies ~16% growth over FY2025's $3.04.

Here's the crux of my rebuttal to the valuation bear: **A PEG of 4.0 is a backward-looking artifact.** It's calculated against historical growth. But if forward EPS growth is genuinely ~16%, the forward PEG collapses toward **1.5** — which is not expensive for a franchise with this margin structure. The bear is anchoring on a multiple that was earned when KO was growing at 2–3%. The company is now growing top-line at 6–7% and bottom-line at double digits. Multiples should expand when the growth rate doubles, and the market is only partially re-rating.

The bear says "the good news is priced in." I say: **the reacceleration is not priced in at all, because consensus is still modeling a staples stalwart.**

---

## 2. The Margin Story Is Genuinely Elite, and Operating Leverage Is Kicking In

This isn't a cost-cutting story — it's a mix-and-pricing story, which is far higher quality:

- **Gross margin: 61.9% TTM, up from 58.1% in FY2022** — a ~380bp expansion.
- **Operating margin: 34.87% TTM** — best-in-class for consumer staples. Almost no large-cap in the sector touches this.
- **Q2'26 operating margin: 35.1%.**

Now overlay the revenue acceleration on top of that margin structure. When a business growing 6–7% top-line is running a 35% operating margin, the incremental-dollar drop-through is enormous — which is exactly why EPS grew 17% on 6.7% revenue growth. **That's the operating leverage the bear conveniently skips over.**

---

## 3. The Balance Sheet Has Been Transformed — This Is a Real, Quantified Deleveraging Story

The bear loves to cite the $43.5B debt figure. That's the gross number. Here's what actually happened over the last twelve months:

| Metric | Q2'25 | Q2'26 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net Debt | ~$39.9B | $30.64B | **−$9.2B (−23%)** |
| Stockholders' Equity | $28.6B | $36.15B | **+26.5%** |
| Tangible Book Value | Negative | **+$8.19B** | Structural swing |

Read that third line again. **Tangible book value went from −$6.58B at FY24 to +$8.19B positive in Q2'26.** That's not a rounding error — that's the balance sheet healing in real time. Net Debt/EBITDA is down to ~1.8x, gross leverage 2.6x, and interest coverage is a fortress-like ~12.0x. Interest expense fell 17% YoY.

The bear's "limited flexibility" claim is stale by about four quarters.

---

## 4. The Cash Flow "Red Flag" Is Actually the Bull's Best Mispricing Argument

This is where I want to push back hardest, because it's the point bears love to lead with — and it's the point most likely to be **backward-looking noise.**

Yes, FY2025 FCF of $5.30B came in below dividends of $8.78B — a 166% payout. But the report itself tells you why:

1. **FY2024's IRS tax deposit (~$6.5B)** crushed prepaid assets (−$5,667M) — a one-time, non-operating timing event.
2. **FY2025's payable paydown (−$7,170M)** was the reverse-swing clean-up.

Now look at what happened once those distortions washed through:

- **Q2'26 operating cash flow: $5.52B, +44.9% YoY.**
- **Q2'26 free cash flow: $5.10B, +51.5% YoY.**
- **H1'26 cumulative OCF ≈ $7.54B — already exceeds the *entire* FY2025 print of $7.41B.**

The business is generating cash at a rate that, if sustained, covers the dividend comfortably. Underlying EBITDA is $18.7B with D&A of just ~$1.05B — this is an asset-light concentrate machine.

And here's the kicker for the bear: **the vendor's headline FCF of $5.22B appears stale — anchored to the FY25 annual, not the trailing four quarters.** Sum the last four quarters and you get **~$14.3B in FCF.** That flips the FCF yield from a misleadingly cheap-looking 1.4% to a genuinely attractive **~3.8%.**

So if the bear's valuation model is built on a stale FCF field, their "expensive" conclusion is built on sand. I'd argue the opposite: **the market may be under-appreciating the true cash generation because a data artifact is understating it.**

## 5. The Competitive Advantages Are About as Durable as They Come

Let me remind the bear what we're actually underwriting here:

- **The single most valuable brand portfolio in non-alcoholic beverages** — concentrate economics, not capital-heavy bottling.
- **Pricing power that has repeatedly held through inflation** — evidenced by the margin expansion above.
- **A 61-year dividend growth streak** — a Dividend King.
- **Beta of 0.34** — a genuine portfolio stabilizer, which in a volatile market is itself a return driver via lower drawdowns and better risk-adjusted compounding.
- **Portfolio pruning that is sharpening the mix:** $3.57B of business sales in FY25 and $5.34B of assets held for sale — management is deliberately exiting lower-margin assets to feed the high-margin concentrate engine.

That last point is underrated. KO isn't just growing — it's **re-mixing** toward higher-quality, higher-margin revenue. That's why the margin runway still exists.

## 6. Now Let Me Take the Bear Arguments Head-On

**Bearer point #1: "Insiders are dumping stock — no 2026 buying."**

Let's be honest about what Form 4s actually show. Nearly every "sale" here is paired with a **"Conversion of Exercise of derivative security" at strikes of $40.89–$61.34.** That's textbook 10b5-1 compensation monetization, not a discretionary bearish bet. Executives at every large-cap sell into strength — that's *why* they're at highs, because the stock went up. Quincey's $145M of sales is enormous in dollar terms precisely *because* the stock is near $90, not because he's running for the exits.

And let's be fair to the other side of the ledger: **Director Levchin bought 14,267 shares at ~$70 in Oct'25.** He's up ~25%. The only conviction signal in the dataset was a *buy*, and it was well-timed. Insider selling is a weak, noisy signal — and it certainly doesn't override a fundamental inflection.

**Bear point #2: "The yield is only 2.41%, below KO's historical ~3%."**

Fair observation — but it's a function of price appreciation, not dividend cuts. KO's dividend has grown for 61 consecutive years. If you want yield, yes, you're paying up. But income investors are buying the *growth of that yield*, and the payout is now far better covered by normalized cash flow than the headline suggests.

**Bear point #3: "GLP-1s and health trends threaten volumes."**

KO has been navigating health-conscious consumers for two decades — through sugar taxes, reformulation pressure, and the entire "war on soda." What has happened? **Gross margins expanded 380bp.** The company pivoted to zero-sugar, sparkling water, sports drinks, coffee, and premium water. This is a portfolio that adapts. And critically, current growth is **price/mix-led** — which is *higher quality* than volume-led because it reflects brand strength.

**Bear point #4: "FX headwinds and the IRS dispute."**

FX translation losses have narrowed from −$15.61B (FY24) to −$12.33B — the *trend is improving*. And the IRS dispute is a known, quantified overhang (the ~$6.5B deposit is already *made*). Any favorable resolution is a **positive catalyst**, not a new risk. The bear is pricing a risk that is already funded.

## The Bottom Line

The bear case is a **valuation snapshot dressed up as a fundamental argument.** Yes, KO trades near 26x trailing earnings. But the company just posted **+6.7% revenue growth, +17% EPS growth, ~35% operating margins, $9.2B of net debt reduction, a swing to positive tangible book value, and a +51% YoY FCF print** — with H1'26 operating cash flow already exceeding all of FY2025.

That is not a mature, 2%-growth business. That's a franchise **accelerating** while its balance sheet repairs and the market's stale FCF data understates its true cash yield.

The bear is right that KO isn't a screaming deep-value trade. But the question isn't "is it cheap?" — it's **"is the improving fundamental trajectory reflected in the price?"** At 25x forward earnings and within 5% of the 52-week high, with the growth reacceleration, deleveraging, and cash-flow inflection all documented, my answer is an unambiguous **no — the market has not fully priced the inflection.**

**I'm a buyer of KO here, adding on any weakness toward the high-$70s, and I'd argue the stock is on a path toward the mid-$90s as the growth story gets recognized.** Bull case stands.
