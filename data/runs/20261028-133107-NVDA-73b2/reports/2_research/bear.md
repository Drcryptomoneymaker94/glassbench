With the **10-Year Treasury yield spiking to ~5.3%** and the Fed Funds Rate at **3.75%**, the discount rate applied to those future $15.91 EPS figures is rising sharply.

Even if NVDA hits its $15.91 EPS target, the *multiple* they are willing to pay for it will compress from historical highs. The "cheap" P/E is not a discount; it’s the market signaling that the risk-adjusted return on equity is no longer justifying the premium multiple.

If AI capex slows by even 10%, NVDA’s revenue decelerates disproportionately, whereas the broader market is diversified. You are not buying "cheap tech"; you are buying a concentrated bet on continued hyper-growth in a hostile rate environment.
